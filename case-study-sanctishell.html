How I Built SanctiShell: My Father's Family Business, Online

Jacob Qumsiyeh · ~4 min read

The Challenge

My father's family has carved Mother-of-Pearl religious pieces by hand in Bethlehem for generations, a craft the Qumsiyeh family workshop has practiced for over fifty years. But the business had no online presence. Every sale depended on foot traffic and word of mouth, and buyers outside the Holy Land had no way to find or purchase the work at all.

The Approach

I built SanctiShell as a real online storefront for the family workshop: a custom-coded WordPress theme on top of WooCommerce, with a full product catalog, cart, and checkout for the collection (crosses, rosaries, religious gifts, and jewelry). I self-host it on my own VPS rather than renting a platform like Shopify, so the business keeps its full margin on every sale with no recurring monthly subscription.

That decision matters for a small family workshop specifically: no per-sale platform cut, no forced pricing tiers, and full control over the storefront as the collection grows.

Design Thinking

The interface stays quiet and product-focused, letting decades of craftsmanship speak for itself rather than competing with it: warm imagery, generous whitespace, and a layout that carries the weight of the tradition without feeling like a generic e-commerce template.

The Result

SanctiShell is live and taking orders, giving my father's business a real online presence for the first time, with zero recurring platform fees cutting into the family's margins on every sale.
